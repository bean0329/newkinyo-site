How to Choose - Three Questions

1. Who is the buyer?

Korean-skincare and hammam audiences buy viscose gloves for real exfoliation. Buyers who describe their skin as sensitive, or who shop for gifts and hotel amenities, prefer sponge mitts. The African net sponge audience is specific and loyal - they search for the product by name and convert well when listings show authentic nylon-net construction and proper length.

2. What is your margin model?

All three lines land in a similar EXW band ($0.28–0.52), so retail positioning decides the margin, not the factory cost. Viscose gloves and net sponges support multi-pack bundles (2-pack, 4-pack) that raise average order value; sponge mitts support premium single-SKU pricing when they carry embroidery or custom density.

3. Can you handle the replenishment rhythm?

Viscose gloves are consumables - buyers reorder every 2–3 months, which is good for repeat revenue but demands stock planning. Net sponges dry fast and last longer, so per-customer reorder is slower but complaint rates are the lowest of the three. Sponge mitts sit in between.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which exfoliating product should a new Amazon seller start with?

Stock-colour viscose gloves or African net sponges - both carry a 100-pc MOQ and the lowest price bands. Viscose suits general bath audiences; net sponges suit the fast-growing African net sponge niche.

Are African net sponges better than viscose gloves?

Neither is strictly better. Net sponges exfoliate harder, dry faster and last longer; viscose gloves offer a more controlled, spa-like exfoliation. Match the product to your audience.

Can I sell all three lines under one brand?

Yes - many clients run 2-3 SKUs across lines with unified packaging. Mixed constructions can be combined in one shipment.

Which line has the lowest return rate?

African net sponges - durable nylon net dries quickly and resists mould, generating the fewest quality complaints of the three lines.
